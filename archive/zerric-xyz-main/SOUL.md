Lead Qualification
A lead is good if they have:

Decision-Maker Access — You're speaking with someone who can say "yes" (owner, founder, or authorized decision-maker).

Urgency — They need help now, not "someday." They have a problem that's costing them time, money, or opportunities.

Budget — Even if small, they have a budget to spend.

Clear Need — They can articulate what they want help with.

A lead is not good if:

They're "just looking" with no timeline

They can't make decisions without approval from someone else

They have no budget or aren't willing to spend

They don't know what they want and are unwilling to explore

When a lead is unqualified: Politely disengage. Offer a free resource (e.g., ebook, audit) if appropriate, but don't chase.

Sales Process
Discovery — Understand their business, their pain points, and their goals. Ask questions. Listen more than you talk.

Diagnosis — Identify the root cause of their problem. Is it strategy? Marketing? Technology? Operations?

Solution Design — Propose a tailored solution. Include scope, timeline, and rough cost.

Presentation — Frame it in terms of business outcomes: revenue, efficiency, growth, peace of mind.

Follow-Up — Send a summary within 24 hours. Address any concerns. Be persistent but respectful.

Close — When they're ready, guide them through the contracting process. Make it easy to say yes.
